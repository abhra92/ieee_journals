### 1. Skin Lesion Classification Using Convolutional Neural Network With Novel Regularizer
**Authors:** M. A. Albahar  
**Affiliation:** Ibn Rushd College for Management Science, Abha, Saudi Arabia  
**[1]** M. A. Albahar, "Skin Lesion Classification Using Convolutional Neural Network With Novel Regularizer," IEEE Access, vol. 7, pp. 38306--38313, 2019, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2906241.

Albahar et al. [1] address the critical clinical challenge of discriminating between benign and malignant skin lesions from dermoscopic imagery, where fine-grained morphological differences complicate automated diagnosis. The author evaluated the framework on dermoscopic images from the benchmark International Skin Imaging Collaboration (ISIC) archive containing 23,906 images across multiple lesion categories. The dataset was partitioned into subsets of approximately 8,000 images, each using a 70% training (5,600 images) and 30% validation split (2,400 images). Preprocessing involved resizing images to 300 × 300 × 3 pixels and applying a power-law transformation (gamma correction) to adjust contrast and reduce computational overhead. The proposed architecture consists of a custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) featuring two convolutional layers embedded with a novel weight-controlling regularizer (λ = 0.02), followed by max pooling, dropout, a 128-neuron dense layer, and an output classification layer. Experimentally, the model achieved an average validation weighted accuracy of 97.49% over 100 epochs. Across binary tasks, the area under the ROC curve reached 0.77 for nevus versus melanoma, 0.93 for seborrheic keratosis versus basal cell carcinoma, 0.85 for seborrheic keratosis versus melanoma, and 0.86 for solar lentigo versus melanoma. Standalone precision, recall, and F1 percentages were not explicitly reported in the text. Key limitations include restricting evaluation to binary pairwise distinctions rather than unified multi-class classification and lack of prospective multi-center validation.

### 2. Region-of-Interest Based Transfer Learning Assisted Framework for Skin Cancer Detection
**Authors:** R. Ashraf, S. Afzal, A. U. Rehman, S. Gul, J. Baber, M. Bakhtyar, I. Mehmood, O. -Y. Song, and M. Maqsood  
**Affiliation:** Department of Computer Science, National Textile University, Faisalabad, Pakistan; Department of Computer Science, COMSATS University Islamabad, Attock, Pakistan  
**[2]** R. Ashraf, S. Afzal, A. U. Rehman, S. Gul, J. Baber, M. Bakhtyar, I. Mehmood, O. -Y. Song, and M. Maqsood, "Region-of-Interest Based Transfer Learning Assisted Framework for Skin Cancer Detection," IEEE Access, vol. 8, pp. 147858--147871, 2020, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3014701.

Ashraf et al. [2] target the diagnostic challenge of differentiating malignant melanoma from visually similar benign melanocytic nevi in dermatoscopic imagery. The authors developed an automated Region-of-Interest (ROI) transfer learning framework evaluated on two clinical benchmarks: the DermIS dataset (705 images: 448 benign and 257 melanoma) and the DermQuest dataset (667 images: 434 nevus and 233 melanoma). Preprocessing employed an improved k-means clustering segmentation algorithm to localize and isolate lesion boundaries from healthy background skin, coupled with offline data augmentation including random rotations, flipping, zooming, and contrast adjustments. Feature extraction and classification were conducted using transfer learning with pre-trained Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures, primarily AlexNet and VGG-16, fine-tuned exclusively on the segmented lesion patches. Quantitative evaluation revealed high diagnostic capability, achieving 97.9% accuracy, 96.3% sensitivity, 98.7% specificity, 96.3% precision, 96.3% F1-score, and an AUC of 0.97 on DermIS. On DermQuest, the model yielded 97.4% accuracy, 95.8% sensitivity, 98.1% specificity, 95.8% precision, 95.8% F1-score, and an AUC of 0.96. The principal strengths lie in eliminating extraneous skin artifacts through localized ROI modeling. However, the study is explicitly limited to binary categorization, relies on moderately sized datasets, and depends on k-means segmentation that may degrade in the presence of severe hair occlusion or diffuse, irregular borders.

### 3. An Interpretable Skin Cancer Classification Using Optimized Convolutional Neural Network for a Smart Healthcare System
**Authors:** K. Mridha, M. M. Uddin, J. Shin, S. Khadka, and M. F. Mridha  
**Affiliation:** Department of Computer Engineering, Marwadi University, Rajkot, Gujarat, India; Department of Computer Science and Engineering, University of Aizu, Aizuwakamatsu, Japan  
**[3]** K. Mridha, M. M. Uddin, J. Shin, S. Khadka, and M. F. Mridha, "An Interpretable Skin Cancer Classification Using Optimized Convolutional Neural Network for a Smart Healthcare System," IEEE Access, vol. 11, pp. 41003--41018, 2023, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3269694.

Addressing the pressing need for interpretable point-of-care dermatology, Mridha et al. [3] develop an explainable deep learning framework integrated into an Android smart healthcare application for automated multi-class skin lesion screening. The authors utilized the benchmark HAM10000 repository comprising 10,015 dermatoscopic images distributed across seven diagnostic classes: actinic keratoses, basal cell carcinoma, benign keratosis-like lesions, dermatofibroma, melanoma, melanocytic nevi, and vascular lesions. To tackle severe class imbalance and dimensional variance, preprocessing comprised image resizing, pixel intensity normalization, and extensive data augmentation incorporating random rotations, zooms, shear transformations, and flips. The proposed architecture is an optimized custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) incorporating batch normalization, max pooling, and dropout, systematically benchmarked across Adam and RMSprop optimizers with ReLU, Swish, and Tanh activation functions. Model interpretability was achieved via Grad-CAM and Grad-CAM++ attribution maps visualizing localized clinical decision regions. The optimized model attained an overall classification accuracy of 82.0%, with a specificity of 94.17%, sensitivity of 82.0%, and a loss accuracy of 0.47%. Key advantages include transparent visual justifications and mobile application integration. Stated limitations include modest multiclass sensitivity on severely underrepresented minority classes (dermatofibroma and vascular lesions) and the requirement for prospective clinical validation on multi-center patient populations.

### 4. Automatic Skin Cancer Detection in Dermoscopy Images Based on Ensemble Lightweight Deep Learning Network
**Authors:** L. Wei, K. Ding, and H. Hu  
**Affiliation:** Anhui Key Laboratory of Electric Drive and Control, Department of Electrical Engineering, Anhui Polytechnic University, Wuhu, China; School of Computer Science and Electric Engineering, University of Essex, Colchester, U.K.  
**[4]** L. Wei, K. Ding, and H. Hu, "Automatic Skin Cancer Detection in Dermoscopy Images Based on Ensemble Lightweight Deep Learning Network," IEEE Access, vol. 8, pp. 99633--99647, 2020, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.2997710.

To resolve the dual bottlenecks of high computational resource consumption and substantial intra-class morphological variance, Wei et al. [4] propose a lightweight recognition and semantic segmentation framework for automated dermoscopic lesion analysis. The authors validated their framework on the benchmark ISBI 2016 challenge dataset (900 training and 379 testing images) and the ISBI 2017 dataset for binary melanoma detection. The preprocessing pipeline integrates a lightweight U-Net segmentation network trained via a transfer learning migration strategy, isolating lesion contours end-to-end without requiring manual artifact filtering. For classification, the architecture introduces a pair-based feature extraction module utilizing lightweight CNNs coupled with a feature discrimination network trained on positive and negative sample pairs using fine-grained classification principles, finalized via ensemble decision fusion. Quantitative testing on ISBI 2016 demonstrated a classification accuracy of 87.3%, an AUC of 0.886, a sensitivity/recall of 83.2%, a specificity of 89.1%, and an average precision of 0.732. The primary architectural merit is the drastic reduction in model parameters while preserving discriminative fine-grained representations. Explicitly declared limitations include sensitivity degradation on lesions with highly indistinct margins, modest ensemble computational overhead during training, and restricting evaluation predominantly to binary classification benchmarks.

Gururaj et al. [5] address the critical clinical need for early cutaneous malignancy detection in resource-constrained settings by introducing 'DeepSkin', a deep learning classification methodology evaluated on the ISIC 2018 and HAM10000 dermatoscopic datasets encompassing 10,015 images distributed across seven pathological categories: melanocytic nevi, melanoma, benign keratosis, basal cell carcinoma, actinic keratoses, vascular lesions, and dermatofibroma. The proposed DeepSkin framework integrates a tailored Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architecture with residual skip connections and comparative transfer learning models (VGG-16, ResNet-50, and MobileNet) to extract multi-level visual representations. Standalone F1-score and AUC metrics were not explicitly reported in the text. ### 6. FCN-Based DenseNet Framework for Automated Detection and Classification of Skin Lesions in Dermoscopy Images
**Authors:** A. A. Adegun and S. Viriri  
**Affiliation:** School of Mathematics, Statistics and Computer Science, University of KwaZulu-Natal, Durban, South Africa  
**[6]** A. A. Adegun and S. Viriri, "FCN-Based DenseNet Framework for Automated Detection and Classification of Skin Lesions in Dermoscopy Images," IEEE Access, vol. 8, pp. 150377--150396, 2020, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2020.3016651.

Adegun et al. [6] tackle the degradation of automated cutaneous malignancy detection caused by fuzzy lesion boundaries, color variation, and artifact presence by introducing an integrated two-stage deep framework combining lesion segmentation and multi-class diagnosis. The system was trained and evaluated on the benchmark ISIC 2017 (2,000 training, 150 validation, 600 test images), ISIC 2018 (2,594 training, 100 validation, 1,000 test images), and DermQuest datasets, categorizing lesions into melanoma, seborrheic keratosis, and benign nevi. Preprocessing included Dull Razor morphological artifact suppression to eliminate occluding hair shafts, followed by contrast enhancement and extensive spatial augmentations (random rotations, affine scaling). The segmentation pipeline utilizes a Fully Convolutional Network (FCN) with encoder-decoder residual skip connections, outputting binary lesion masks that crop input patches for a deep DenseNet classification model incorporating dense connectivity blocks and transition layers. The FCN segmentation achieved a 98.5% accuracy, 92.5% Dice coefficient, and 86.8% Jaccard index. For classification, the framework achieved 95.0% accuracy on ISIC 2017 and 98.5% on ISIC 2018, with a sensitivity of 96.0%, specificity of 97.0%, F1-score of 95.0%, and an AUC of 0.98. Stated limitations include high computational resource demands during dual-stage training and slight boundary over-segmentation on lesions with poorly demarcated, diffuse margins.

### 8. Skin Cancer Detection Using Combined Decision of Deep Learners
**Authors:** A. Imran, A. Nasir, M. Bilal, G. Sun, A. Alzahrani, and A. Almuhaimeed  
**Affiliation:** Faculty of Information Technology, Beijing University of Technology, Beijing, China; Department of Computer Science and Information Technology, Superior University, Lahore, Pakistan  
**[8]** A. Imran, A. Nasir, M. Bilal, G. Sun, A. Alzahrani, and A. Almuhaimeed, "Skin Cancer Detection Using Combined Decision of Deep Learners," IEEE Access, vol. 10, pp. 118198--118212, 2022, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3220329.

Imran et al. [8] address the challenge of distinguishing malignant melanoma from benign skin lesions where subtle chromatic similarities cause high false-positive rates by designing a combined decision ensemble framework evaluated on a curated, balanced subset of 2,800 dermoscopic images from the ISIC Archive (comprising 1,400 benign and 1,400 malignant cases). Preprocessing involved image normalization, resizing to 224 × 224 pixels, and data augmentation including random rotations, horizontal/vertical flips, and scaling to improve model generalization. The proposed hybrid framework fuses predictions from three distinct deep learning architectures: a customized VGG-16, a residual ResNet-50, and a Capsule Network (CapsNet) capable of modeling spatial hierarchy and orientation relationships. Final predictions are produced through an ensemble soft-voting decision mechanism. Experimentally, the combined decision framework achieved an overall classification accuracy of 93.50%, a sensitivity of 87.25%, a specificity of 94.80%, a precision of 94.30%, and an F1-score of 90.64%. The standalone AUC was not explicitly reported in the text. Key strengths include superior discriminative reliability over individual learners. Stated limitations include the computational overhead of simultaneously running three deep networks during inference, evaluation restricted to a balanced binary dataset, and the lack of validation on diverse multi-class dermatological conditions.

### 9. FixCaps: An Improved Capsules Network for Diagnosis of Skin Cancer
**Authors:** Z. Lan, S. Cai, X. He, and X. Wen  
**Affiliation:** School of Automation, Guangdong University of Technology, Guangzhou, China  
**[9]** Z. Lan, S. Cai, X. He, and X. Wen, "FixCaps: An Improved Capsules Network for Diagnosis of Skin Cancer," IEEE Access, vol. 10, pp. 76261--76267, 2022, doi: 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3181225.

Lan et al. [9] introduce 'FixCaps', an improved capsule network architecture designed for automated dermoscopic diagnosis that overcomes the loss of spatial hierarchies in traditional CNNs and the iterative dynamic routing latency of standard Capsule Networks. The authors evaluated FixCaps on the benchmark HAM10000 repository comprising 10,015 images spanning seven dermatological classes: melanoma, melanocytic nevi, basal cell carcinoma, actinic keratoses, benign keratosis, dermatofibroma, and vascular lesions. Preprocessing included color normalization, image resizing, and affine data augmentation. FixCaps replaces traditional dynamic routing with a fixed matrix transformation routing mechanism and integrates convolutional attention modules, drastically accelerating training convergence while retaining spatial transformation equivariance and vector feature representations. Quantitative experiments demonstrated that FixCaps achieved a peak classification accuracy of 96.49%, a sensitivity of 93.47%, a specificity of 98.13%, a precision of 93.64%, an F1-score of 93.55%, and an AUC of 0.98. Key advantages include superior parameter efficiency and preservation of spatial lesion features. However, the authors explicitly noted limitations: computational latency remains higher than standard lightweight CNNs, minor performance degradation occurs on heavily underrepresented minority classes such as dermatofibroma, and the model was not tested on independent clinical photography.

### 10. Deep Clustering via Center-Oriented Margin Free-Triplet Loss for Skin Lesion Detection in Highly Imbalanced Datasets

**Affiliation:** Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering, Bilkent University, Ankara, Turkey; National Magnetic Resonance Research Center (UMRAM), Bilkent University, Ankara, Turkey  
**[10]** Ş. Öztürk and T. Çukur, "Deep Clustering via Center-Oriented Margin Free-Triplet Loss for Skin Lesion Detection in Highly Imbalanced Datasets," IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics, vol. 26, pp. 4679--4690, 2022, doi: 10.1109/JBHI.2022.3187215.

Öztürk et al. [10] address severe sample imbalance between common benign lesions and rare malignant melanoma by proposing a deep metric clustering framework trained with a novel Center-Oriented Margin-Free Triplet Loss (COMF-TL). The method was rigorously validated on the ISIC 2018 (10,015 images across 7 classes) and ISIC 2019 (25,331 images across 8 classes) challenge benchmarks. Preprocessing involved color standardization, center-cropping, and random affine transformations. COMF-TL adaptively pulls feature embeddings toward dynamically updated class cluster centers while repelling negative clusters without requiring a manually tuned fixed margin hyperparameter. Implemented on VGG-16 and ResNet-50 backbones, the deep clustering framework attained a balanced multi-class accuracy of 83.7%, an overall AUC of 0.912 (improving AUC by 4.58% over standard cross-entropy baselines), a sensitivity of 82.4%, a specificity of 92.1%, and an F1-score of 81.9%. Precision was not explicitly reported as a standalone percentage in the text. The core strength is robust boundary separation in severely skewed feature spaces. Stated limitations include elevated training times caused by continuous cluster center recalculation and triplet mining, along with evaluation restricted to dermoscopic archives without prospective clinical deployment.

### 13. EFAM-Net: A Multi-Class Skin Lesion Classification Model Utilizing Enhanced Feature Fusion and Attention Mechanisms

Experimentally, EFAM-Net achieved a classification accuracy of 92.30% on ISIC 2019, with a balanced accuracy of 90.07%, precision of 90.25%, recall/sensitivity of 89.80%, F1-score of 90.02%, and an AUC of 0.960. On HAM10000, accuracy reached 93.85%. The primary strength is its superior discriminative capacity across visually overlapping categories.
